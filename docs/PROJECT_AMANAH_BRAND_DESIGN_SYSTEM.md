# Project Amanah — Brand & Design System

**Version:** 1.2  
**Brand:** Project Amanah  
**Tagline:** Monitoring Anti-Muslim Hate Online

> **Open-datapack UI rule (2026-08-22):** For reviewed Kaggle/other open datapack records, label platform/source as `N/A`; show Dataset provider/name/version as separate provenance, never as a substitute platform source. See the authoritative root [`spec.md`](../spec.md).

## 1. Brand foundation

### Name

Use **Project Amanah** on first reference and **Amanah** thereafter. *Amānah* means a trust, responsibility or something placed in one’s care. The name is not decorative: the system is entrusted with sensitive evidence, the dignity of the people represented in it, the wellbeing of reviewers, and the obligation to speak carefully and justly.

### Positioning

Project Amanah is a research and community-safety observatory that helps qualified users detect, understand, review, and document public anti-Muslim hate online. It tracks patterns and narratives; it is not a mass-surveillance product, a censorship engine, or a tool for profiling Muslims.

### Brand promise

**Turn fragmented public signals into careful, reviewable evidence.**

### Primary message

> Understand how anti-Muslim hate moves online.

Project Amanah monitors public online conversations and visual content to help researchers and communities identify harmful narratives, changes over time, and temporal associations with public events.

### Message pillars

1. **Detect responsibly:** purpose-built text and multimodal analysis with uncertainty visible.
2. **Understand patterns:** narratives, propagation, trends, and spikes—not just raw counts.
3. **Keep humans accountable:** decisions remain reviewable, correctable, and auditable.
4. **Preserve evidence carefully:** provenance, integrity, access controls, and retention rules are first-class.

### Why the product is useful

An individual hateful comment is upsetting but easy to dismiss as isolated. A longitudinal, reviewable observatory can show whether the same narrative is recurring, where it is appearing, whether its rate is changing, what public events coincide with that change, and which evidence deserves human attention. Project Amanah helps community organizations, researchers and advocates:

- replace anecdotal impressions with scoped, qualified evidence;
- detect narrative shifts and unusual increases earlier;
- preserve context for research, briefings, platform reports and policy work;
- prioritize limited review capacity without automating final judgment;
- reduce the need for staff to repeatedly search for and reread harmful material;
- learn from reviewer corrections and measure whether patterns persist or change.

The product does not promise to eliminate hate. Its value is making a fragmented, emotionally exhausting problem more legible, traceable and actionable.

### Founding perspective

An optional founder-story block may speak in a genuine first-person voice:

> “Many of us know the shock of encountering anti-Muslim hate online. Repeated exposure can become fatigue, numbness or normalization—but the harm has not disappeared. Project Amanah helps communities recognize the pattern without asking people to carry every incident alone.”

Use this as a personal/community perspective, not as a claim that all Muslims respond in the same way. Pair it with agency and purpose; do not use trauma as marketing spectacle.

### Faith-rooted narrative

**Core idea:** *A trust we carry together.*

Islam frames trust as inseparable from responsibility and justice. [Qur’an 4:58](https://quran.com/4/58) connects rendering trusts to their rightful place with judging fairly. [Qur’an 3:104](https://quran.com/3/104) calls for a community that invites to goodness, encourages what is right and resists what is wrong. [Qur’an 9:71](https://quran.com/9/71) describes believing women and men as allies/protectors of one another who encourage good and forbid evil.

For Project Amanah, these principles become design obligations:

- **Trust:** handle evidence, identities, access and claims as things placed in our care.
- **Mutual care:** the wellbeing and dignity of one another are a shared responsibility, not somebody else’s problem.
- **Enjoining good:** support truth, justice, education, solidarity, careful advocacy and human accountability.
- **Forbidding wrong:** document and resist harm through lawful, ethical, evidence-led means—not harassment, vigilantism or coercion.
- **Ghayrah for the deen:** maintain a disciplined protective concern that refuses indifference when Islam or Muslims are demeaned, while remaining governed by truth, mercy, wisdom and justice.

*Ghayrah* may be spelled “gheerah” in audience-facing copy when that spelling is more familiar. Never translate it as rage, possessiveness or a license to police individuals. Project Amanah expresses ghayrah by taking harm seriously and responding responsibly.

Recommended story paragraph:

> **Amanah is a trust.** We are entrusted with the care of one another and called to stand for what is good and resist what causes harm. Project Amanah carries that responsibility into the digital public square: making anti-Muslim hate visible, contextual and reviewable so communities can respond with evidence, wisdom and justice—without surrendering anyone’s dignity.

Have final sacred-language copy reviewed by a trusted scholar or qualified community advisor. The product should be openly faith-rooted without presenting itself as a religious authority.

## 2. Voice and tone

The voice is **faith-rooted, calm, precise, humane, nonpartisan, and evidence-led**. Write for Muslim communities first while remaining understandable to researchers, advocates, journalists, partners and policy practitioners without requiring Islamic-studies or ML expertise.

- Say **“classified as likely anti-Muslim hate”**, not “is hate,” until reviewed.
- Say **“temporally associated with”** or **“coincides with,”** never “caused by,” unless causal evidence exists.
- Say **“confidence”** or **“model score,”** not “certainty.”
- Describe conduct and narratives, not people as inherently dangerous.
- Avoid sensational language, gamified alerts, and decontextualized slur repetition.
- Explain Islamic terms in plain English on first use; never use sacred language as ornament or a slogan detached from product conduct.
- Use content warnings before exposed hateful material.
- Never imply that Muslim identity, Islamic symbols, Arabic text, mosques, hijab, or religious discussion indicate hate. They indicate relevance at most.

### Product vocabulary

- **Amanah Signals:** noteworthy changes or items
- **Amanah Trends:** time-series views
- **Amanah Narratives:** clustered themes and frames
- **Amanah Review:** human adjudication queue
- **Evidence Record:** integrity-preserved source snapshot and metadata
- **Meme Signals:** visual-narrative and propagation view
- **A trust we carry together:** primary faith-rooted story line
- **Ghayrah:** disciplined protective concern for the deen and community; never anger, vigilantism or person-level policing

## 3. Logo direction

Create a restrained wordmark plus a simple symbol. Recommended symbol: an abstract, open geometric form combining a **shield/arch** (care and protection) with a **signal/ripple** (observation). It must not resemble a police badge, target reticle, eye, mosque silhouette, crescent-and-star, national emblem, or Arabic calligraphy used merely as ornament.

Logo variants:

- Primary horizontal: symbol + “Project Amanah” + optional tagline
- Compact: symbol + “Amanah”
- Mark-only: favicon/app icon, only after recognition is established
- One-color: navy on light, white on dark

Clear space: at least the height of the capital **A** on every side. Minimum digital widths: 140 px with tagline, 96 px without, 24 px mark-only. Never stretch, rotate, add shadows, recolor outside the approved palette, or place over busy hate-content imagery.

## 5. Typography

Use open-source, broadly available families:

- **Headings and UI:** [Inter](https://rsms.me/inter/) — 600/700
- **Editorial summaries:** [Source Serif 4](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Source+Serif+4) — 400/600
- **Data and identifiers:** [IBM Plex Mono](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/IBM+Plex+Mono) — 400/500

Fallbacks: `Inter, ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif`; `Source Serif 4, Georgia, serif`; `IBM Plex Mono, ui-monospace, monospace`.

Base body text is 16 px with 1.5 line height; minimum UI label is 14 px. Use tabular numerals for metrics. Avoid all caps for sentences and do not place hateful text in display typography.

## 6. UI principles

1. **Context before verdict:** show source, surrounding discussion, target, rationale, and model/version with every classification.
2. **Uncertainty is visible:** confidence bands and review state appear beside labels, not in tooltips.
3. **Rates before raw totals:** pair counts with denominators, collection coverage, and missing-data warnings.
4. **Progressive exposure:** blur or collapse hateful imagery/text by default; require an intentional reveal.
5. **Review is reversible:** corrections retain history and never silently overwrite model output.
6. **No vanity metrics:** avoid celebratory animations, leaderboards, or red “scoreboards” of hate.
7. **Accessible density:** compact research views may be dense, but hierarchy, spacing, and keyboard paths remain clear.
8. **Traceable insights:** every aggregate drills down to supporting, access-controlled records.

## 7. Marketing site structure

The page should read as one natural argument: meaning of amanah → shared responsibility → lived problem → missing capability → responsible solution → concrete workflow → evidence of usefulness → limits → login.

1. **Header:** wordmark; Why Amanah; How it works; Responsible use; Methodology; “Log in.”
2. **Hero:** overline “A trust we carry together”; headline “Understand how anti-Muslim hate moves online.” Supporting line: “Project Amanah turns authorized public signals into trends, narratives, context and reviewable reports—without profiling people.” Actions: “See how it works” and “Log in.”
3. **Meaning of Amanah:** explain that care for one another, truthfulness and justice are responsibilities placed in our care. Introduce enjoining good, resisting wrong and ghayrah in clear, restrained language.
4. **Human problem:** isolated incidents disappear into feeds; repeated exposure creates burden and can normalize what should remain concerning. Use the founder perspective sparingly.
5. **Capability gap:** platforms show posts; communities need longitudinal patterns, context, evidence and a path from signal to careful review.
6. **Three questions:** How much is appearing in the monitored sample? What narratives and forms are present? What changed around it?
7. **System walkthrough:** Capture → Classify → Contextualize → Route to human review → Learn and report. Make clear that “route” means an internal review queue and “decide” remains human.
8. **Product proof:** one synthetic/redacted story moves from a chart spike to filtered evidence, a reviewer decision and a scoped report export.
9. **Meme intelligence:** explain image + text + post-context analysis and near-duplicate candidates.
10. **Community and event context:** monitored communities, candidate-community discovery, and local/global news overlays; label source coverage and non-causal associations.
11. **Responsible-use section:** show how amanah governs authorized access, evidence stewardship, synthetic/redacted demo material, no identity profiling, privacy, non-causal event language and model limitations.
12. **Methodology and disclosure:** datasets, AI tools, licenses, evaluation, earlier work, community consultation and changelog.
13. **Final call to action:** “Carry the trust with care” → Log in.
14. **Footer:** contact, security, privacy, terms, accessibility, repository (if public).

Do not show actual hateful content on the public landing page. Use synthetic redacted examples.

The marketing site and authenticated dashboard may share one Netlify application, but they are visually distinct route groups. Public routes use editorial storytelling and synthetic/redacted previews. After login, the interface changes to a compact research workspace; raw-content, search, Insights and review routes require authentication. Open registration is disabled for the hackathon.

## 8. Dashboard design language

### Navigation

Overview · Explorer · Insights · Narratives · Meme Signals · Review

Utility navigation: Reports · Sources & Coverage · Connections · Settings · Methodology · Log out

### Overview hierarchy

- Coverage strip: sources active, records observed, collection window, last successful run
- Primary trend: likely anti-Muslim rate with reviewed subset and uncertainty band
- Signal cards: spike magnitude, dominant narrative, affected sources, review status
- Severity distribution: animosity, derogation, dehumanization, threat
- Event overlay: visually distinct annotations explicitly labeled “temporal association”
- Review workload: queued, urgent, disagreement, completed

### Components

- **Signal card:** title, plain-language reason, change vs baseline, evidence count, review badge
- **Classification badge:** `Model only`, `Needs review`, `Human confirmed`, `Human rejected`
- **Evidence drawer:** content warning, source context, captured-at time, integrity hash, model rationale, reviewer history
- **Chart:** descriptive title, visible units/denominator, hover + keyboard values, exportable data, missing-period gaps
- **Meme family card:** blurred representative image, occurrences, platforms, first/last seen, near-duplicate confidence
- **Report builder:** active-filter summary, included sections, coverage/methodology disclosure, redaction mode and export action
- **Community candidate card:** source-scoped community/channel, discovery reason, sample size, risk/coverage note and `Approve`, `Dismiss` or `Needs review`; never label a community inherently hateful

Use red only within reviewed severity states. A rising chart uses teal/gold until a high-risk interpretation is confirmed.

## 9. Accessibility and trauma-aware use

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic landmarks, 4.5:1 body-text contrast, 3:1 large text/UI graphics, 44×44 px touch targets where practical.
- Never encode meaning using color alone. Provide patterns, icons, and text.
- Charts need text summaries and downloadable tabular equivalents.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; no flashing or pulsing alerts.
- Hateful content is hidden by default with content type and severity disclosed before reveal.
- Provide “hide all media,” “redact slurs,” and session-level exposure controls.
- Avoid unnecessary autoplay, infinite scroll, and forced repeated exposure.
- Arabic and right-to-left content must render with correct `dir`, Unicode-safe search, and suitable fallback fonts.
- Images require useful alt text; hateful meme alt text should summarize the mechanism without gratuitously reproducing abuse.

## 10. Usage rules

### Do

- Pair Project Amanah with the tagline on first public appearance.
- Attribute model-derived claims and clearly mark reviewed claims.
- Publish methodology, coverage gaps, known biases, and version history.
- Use synthetic/redacted samples in public communications.
- Frame community discovery as a reviewable sampling decision, not automated suspicion.
- Let the meaning of amanah show through restraint, justice, privacy and accountability—not only faith-language copy.

### Do not

- Use the brand to endorse automated takedowns or identify “problem users.”
- rank individuals, infer religion, or build dossiers.
- imply comprehensive platform coverage.
- display hateful screenshots without access controls and warnings.
- frame ordinary Muslim content as suspicious.
- imply that a discovered subreddit, channel or community is collectively hateful because some sampled content matched.
- use “forbidding evil” to imply coercion, vigilantism, harassment, automated punishment or judging another person’s faith.
- use ghayrah to excuse anger, sensationalism or careless claims.

## 11. Canonical microcopy

- Empty state: “No signals meet these filters. This does not mean no anti-Muslim hate occurred.”
- Model disclaimer: “Machine-generated assessment. Review context before use or publication.”
- Event label: “News activity in the same period; no causal claim is implied.”
- Reveal control: “Show potentially harmful content”
- Review correction: “Your decision is saved with the original model output in the audit history.”
- Report export: “This report reflects the selected sources, dates and filters—not the whole platform.”
- Community candidate: “Suggested for analyst review; monitoring is not active.”
- Source limitation: “Access unavailable or not approved. No scraping fallback was used.”
- Faith-rooted overline: “A trust we carry together.”
- Amanah statement: “Entrusted with evidence. Accountable to truth, dignity and justice.”
- Closing action: “Carry the trust with care.”
